# The prompt's ideal shape — the hindsight-20/20 derivation (2026-09-04)

Owner directive: "what should the prompt look like if we didn't care about
the effort to get there — then get there." This is the from-scratch
derivation, written BEFORE reading the red-team agent's findings so the
two can check each other.

## What tonight proved about this document's failure physics

Every measured seesaw (host-venue↔shelf, correction↔withdrawal,
counter↔store, happy-hour↔affordable, ONE-subject↔shared-verb) had the
same anatomy: **one underlying question spelled in two or more places in
different words.** Under context pressure the model picks a spelling, and
which one it picks varies per run. Every fix that worked was the same
move: name the ONE question, state it once, make the old sites cases or
pointers. The ideal shape is therefore not "better wording" — it is
**minimum spellings**: every law stated exactly once, everything else a
case of some law.

## The seven laws (derived from the job, not from the current text)

The job: from community text, transcribe exactly the claims a food
searcher can use — (place, dish?, attributes, endorsement), each the act
of a specific writer.

1. **THE ACT LAW** — every clause is a writer DOING something (verdict,
   fact, plan, ask, hearsay, steer, pick, affirmation, announcement,
   closure, self-promo) to ONE OR MORE subjects. Genre does not exist;
   only clauses act. (Today: A.0 — sound.)
2. **THE LANDING LAW** — a subject's verdict is where its own clauses
   land; every clause reaches every subject its verb covers; a defeated
   subject contributes NOTHING, not even its adjectives. (Today: A.0's
   landing + baseline + NEGATIVE CONTENT + Gate 3's vouching-clause rule
   — the "defeated clause emits nothing" consequence is spelled at least
   four times. One law, one statement, three pointers.)
3. **THE CHOICE LAW** — choosing to hand a thing to someone else's want
   endorses exactly what was chosen. Its four sub-questions, today
   scattered across the volunteer principle / ANSWER TEST / ONE QUESTION
   / deal posture / label-vs-thing: whose want (never your own, never
   your business), what would make an answer WRONG (judgment vs fact),
   what was actually handed (the label-vs-thing test), and the handing
   posture (recommending vs reporting). These read today as five
   mechanisms; they are one law with four questions. The largest
   remaining consolidation, and the riskiest — this region is the most
   recently owner-ruled and the most example-dense. Consolidate LAST,
   with the full deck as the gate.
4. **THE MAKER TEST** — who is behind this food, and does it reach the
   eater as a served plate or a good carried off. (Done 2026-09-03.)
5. **THE STATUS TEST** — text-stated gone-ness kills the subject for the
   whole post object. (Done 2026-09-03.)
6. **THE QUOTATION LAW** — the deepest unifier still unclaimed: **you
   are a transcriptionist, never a knower.** Every emitted string is a
   QUOTE of this source — names as written (B.3), dishes as spoken
   (C.2/C.3), attributes only from describing words (D), fit words only
   as the ask wrote them (D), cuisine never inferred (D.4), world
   knowledge never used (B.3, D.4), typos sacred, diacritics identity,
   translation forbidden. Today spelled independently at least SIX
   times. Ideal: stated once, early, as the document's identity ("what
   you produce is evidence — a transcript of choices — for downstream
   judges; anything you add from outside the text poisons the
   evidence"), with the six sites becoming per-field mechanics.
7. **THE PLURALITY LAW** — everything applicable, nothing twice: fan-out
   across subjects, every constraint word, unions on collapse, dedupe
   within arrays. (Seeded 2026-09-03; should be stated once beside the
   quotation law rather than patched into four sites.)

## The ideal document

- **Part I — THE SEVEN LAWS** (~1 page): each law, its one-line test, its
  one canonical example. This is what the model should be able to hold
  in attention for every clause.
- **Part II — THE PIPELINE (A–F)**: unchanged in ORDER (the pipeline
  works; execution order is not the disease), but every step becomes
  METHOD — how to apply the laws to this field — with pointers to Part I
  instead of restatements. Examples stay with their methods (they are
  the pins), but each example cites its law.
- **What gets deleted**: every second+ spelling of laws 2, 3, 6, 7.
  Expected net shrink ~15–25% while INCREASING coverage — the same trade
  every unification tonight made.

## Downstream list handling — VERIFIED ARRAY-FAITHFUL (2026-09-04)

projections with line evidence: per-element everywhere, entity-id-keyed

## The expedition lesson (2026-09-04) — a method law

The one 'ambient flaky' pin, expedition-ordered by the owner, was three
stacked doctrine gaps (hearsay-never-lands, ask-doubt-never-un-says,
scale/ownership-non-evaluative). Then same-hour ablation against the
committed baseline attributed three MORE 'ambient' flakies to real edit
side-effects (fit sweeping dish words; probe zero eating cravings; a
'stays short' example teaching name truncation). Method law going
forward: a full-deck flaky is 'ambient' only after it passes on the
committed baseline AND fails on the candidate in the SAME hour; the deck
alone cannot distinguish variance from a regression. Result: the first
284/284 x3 all-pass deck of the campaign.

Wave-1..3 hoists (quotation / passing-clause / deal-law / seven-laws
header) remain queued as v24; the choice-law consolidation last.
